# Task script

Say the words in quotes. Do not explain the screens, define words, or help unless the participant is stuck for two minutes; then note it as "helped".

## A. The first two minutes

Open the app on Picker. "This person is Ayanda, a made-up shop owner. Please open her, then just look at the first screen for a moment. Think out loud: say whatever you notice or wonder."

Tap Ayanda, Agree and continue, then hand the phone over. Time two minutes. Note the first thing they look at, every word they say, and anything they tap.

At two minutes: "In your own words, what is this screen telling Ayanda?"

## B. Three tasks

Run T1, T2 and T3 in that order in sessions 1, 3 and 5, and T3, T1, T2 in sessions 2, 4 and 6, so learning from one task does not always help the next.

**T1. When does the money run out?** (Thabo)
Switch to Thabo. "Thabo drives for a ride app. Using the app, tell me when Thabo's money will run out, and how sure the app is."
Success: names the headline date and the likely range or confidence in their own words.

**T2. What should Thabo do today?** (Thabo, tests the date-first hypothesis)
Before the session, note Thabo's daily budget on Home and set the amount X to about one and a half times it, rounded to R10, so the answer is not obvious.
Ask T2 twice, once on a balance-only screen and once on Home. The balance-only screen is Money, scrolled so the balance and transactions show and the forecast does not. Sessions 1, 3 and 5 start on Money; sessions 2, 4 and 6 start on Home.
"Thabo wants to spend about RX today on airtime, lunch and a taxi. Is that okay? Show me how you decided."
Success on Home: uses the daily budget or the state, not only the balance, and does not read the date as a deadline to spend up to. Note whether the answer changed between the two screens and why.

**T3. A loan to family** (Lerato)
Switch to Lerato. "Lerato's cousin asks to borrow R500 until next week. Use the app to help her decide."
Success: finds Plan, enters R500, reads the change in date or state, and notices the line about lending only what she could manage without.

After each task: "How easy or hard was that, from 1 (very hard) to 5 (very easy)? Why?"

## C. Wrap-up

"What would you tell a friend this app does?"
"Is there anything on these screens you would not want someone else to see on your phone?"
"If someone asked you for money, would you show them this screen? Why or why not?"
"Would you use something like this? What would stop you?"

Thank the participant and give the thank-you.
